## Explanation

The array itself is not something we're binary-searching. Instead, we're binary-searching the possible value of k.

1. First understand the question

Suppose:

piles = [3,6,7,11]
h = 8

Koko chooses one fixed speed k.

If:

k = 4

then:

pile 3  → 1 hour
pile 6  → 2 hours
pile 7  → 2 hours
pile 11 → 3 hours

Total:

1 + 2 + 2 + 3 = 8 hours

So k = 4 works.

Can we use k = 3?

3  → 1 hour
6  → 2 hours
7  → 3 hours
11 → 4 hours

Total:

1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10

Too slow.

Therefore answer is 4.

2. Where does binary search come from?

Look at what happens as k increases.

For example:

k = 1   → takes a HUGE number of hours
k = 2   → fewer hours
k = 3   → fewer hours
k = 4   → 8 hours
k = 5   → <= 8 hours
k = 6   → <= 8 hours
...
k = 11  → <= 8 hours

So the answers have this pattern:

k:        1  2  3  4  5  6  7  ... 11
works?    ❌  ❌  ❌  ✅  ✅  ✅  ✅ ... ✅

That's the key!

We have a monotonic property:

If speed k can finish all bananas in h hours, then every speed greater than k can also finish them.

So we're looking for the first k that works.

That is exactly what binary search is good at.

3. What are we binary-searching?

Possible speed:

1 <= k <= max(piles)

Why?

Minimum

k = 0 makes no sense, so minimum is:

1
Maximum

We never need to eat faster than the largest pile.

For:

[3,6,7,11]

k = 11 is definitely enough because every pile finishes in one hour.

So:

left = 1;
right = *max_element(piles.begin(), piles.end());
4. How do we check whether a speed works?

For one pile:

pile = 11
k = 4

We need:

ceil(11 / 4) = 3 hours

In integer arithmetic:

(pile + k - 1) / k

So total hours for speed k:

hours += (pile + k - 1) / k;

Then:

hours <= h

means k is fast enough.

5. The binary search

Suppose:

piles = [3,6,7,11]
h = 8

Search range:

left = 1
right = 11
mid = 6

Check speed 6:

3  → 1
6  → 1
7  → 2
11 → 2

total = 6

6 <= 8, so 6 works.

But we want the minimum speed.

Therefore:

right = mid - 1

Search:

1 ... 5
mid = 3
3  → 1
6  → 2
7  → 3
11 → 4

total = 10

10 > 8.

Too slow.

Therefore we need a larger speed:

left = mid + 1

Search:

4 ... 5
mid = 4
3  → 1
6  → 2
7  → 2
11 → 3

total = 8

Works.

So:

right = 3

Now:

left = 4
right = 3

Stop.

Answer = 4.
