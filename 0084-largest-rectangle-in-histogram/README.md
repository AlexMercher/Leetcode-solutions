## Explanation

2. The current height
int curr = (i == n ? 0 : heights[i]);

Normally:

curr = heights[i];

But when i == n, we pretend there is a bar of height 0.

Why?

Imagine:

[2,1,5,6]

At the end, 5 and 6 may still be sitting in the stack.

There is no actual smaller bar after them, so nothing would trigger their removal.

The fake:

0

forces everything remaining out of the stack.

So the last iteration is essentially:

[2,1,5,6,0]

The 0 is called a sentinel.

3. The while loop — find the right boundary
while(!st.empty() && heights[st.top()] > curr)

This is the most important part.

Suppose:

stack top = height 6
curr = 2

Since:

6 > 2

the bar of height 6 cannot extend any further right.

Therefore we pop it:

int h = heights[st.top()];
st.pop();

At this exact moment:

i is the first position to the right where the height is smaller than h.

So i is the right boundary.

That's why later we use:

right = i

even though we don't explicitly store a variable called right.

4. Find the left boundary

After popping:

int left = st.empty() ? -1 : st.top();

This is very clever.

Because the stack is increasing, after popping the current bar:

st.top()

is the nearest bar to the left that is smaller than h.

So:

left = index of previous smaller bar

If the stack is empty:

left = -1;

which means:

There is no smaller bar on the left, so the rectangle can extend all the way to index 0.

5. Calculate the width
int width = i - left - 1;

Why -1?

Because both boundaries themselves are smaller bars and cannot belong to the rectangle.

Suppose:

left = 1
right = 5

Then the usable indices are:

2,3,4

So:

width = 5 - 1 - 1
      = 3

Hence:

ans = max(ans, width * h);

We just found the largest rectangle whose limiting height is h.

Let's connect all 5 chunks

Take:

heights = [2,1,5,6,2,3]

When we encounter 2 after 6:

... 5 6 2
      ↑ ↑

The 2 tells us:

"6 can no longer continue."

So pop 6.

The stack now tells us the previous smaller bar.

Suppose:

left = 2
right = 4
height = 6

Then:

width = 4 - 2 - 1
      = 1

Area:

6 × 1 = 6

Then 5 may also be popped:

height = 5
left = 1
right = 4

So:

width = 4 - 1 - 1
      = 2

Area:

5 × 2 = 10

And that's the famous maximum for this example.

The entire algorithm in one sentence

For every bar:

Wait until you encounter a smaller bar; that tells you exactly where the current bar's rectangle must stop on the right, while the stack tells you where it stopped on the left. Then calculate its maximum possible rectangle.
