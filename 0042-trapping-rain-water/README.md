## Explanation

For any position:

water[i] = min(max wall on left, max wall on right) - height[i]

For example:

left wall = 5
right wall = 8
current height = 2

water = min(5,8) - 2
      = 3

The shorter boundary determines how much water can stay.

What are leftMax and rightMax?

They represent:

leftMax  = highest wall we've seen from the left
rightMax = highest wall we've seen from the right

So:

leftMax=max(leftMax,height[left]);

updates the best boundary on the left.

Similarly:

rightMax=max(rightMax,height[right]);

updates the best boundary on the right.

The important condition
if(leftMax <= rightMax)

means:

The best wall we currently know on the left is no taller than the best wall we know on the right.

Therefore, for the current left position, the right side already has a boundary at least as tall as the left boundary.

So the limiting side is definitely the left side.

Hence:

water += leftMax - height[left];

is safe.

Why?

Suppose:

leftMax  = 5
rightMax = 8

Then:

min(leftMax, rightMax)
= min(5,8)
= 5

So we know the current position's water depends on 5.

We don't need to know the exact tallest wall on the entire right side. We already know there is a right boundary of at least 8, which is enough.

What if leftMax > rightMax?

Then the right side is the limiting side.

For example:

leftMax  = 10
rightMax = 6

Then:

min(10,6) = 6

So we can safely calculate water at right:

water += rightMax - height[right];

That's exactly what the else does.

The line to really memorize is:
if(leftMax <= rightMax)

not as a magic condition, but as:

“Whichever side currently has the smaller boundary can be finalized.”
